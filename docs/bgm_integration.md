# Runtime BGM integration

Exploration, battle and fanfare use Pyxel channels 0–2; office music uses only channels 0–1. All sound effects use channel 3. There are no hidden overdubs. Sound slots 32–40 contain the three field arrangements; slots 41–42 contain the two office parts. Existing effect slots are retained.

Exploration uses the approved bright forest jazz arrangement of Schumann, Waldszenen Op.82 No.3 (100 BPM, 19.2-second loop). Combat uses the approved rock arrangement (138.461538 BPM, 13.866667-second loop). The original celebratory fanfare lasts 2.25 seconds. Score data is editable in src/drift_with_me/data/bgm_score.json. These are our own Pyxel note sequences, not recordings or third-party MIDI. The historical score reference is the Clara Schumann edited Breitkopf & Härtel 1887 edition, plate R.S.70, page 5: https://imslp.org/wiki/Waldszenen,_Op.82_(Schumann,_Robert). Historical source inspection was completed during the arrangement study; no new modern recording or arrangement was imported.

Combat entry starts the rock track alongside the channel-3 clash, followed by the position-settling noise. At each normal-enemy parry round, music phase is aligned to the first actual target. The quarter note is 26/60 seconds: normal targets are 0.42 seconds apart plus approximately one 60 Hz hitstop frame. This is a controlled normal-enemy approximation, not a universal rhythm guarantee. Frame delays, misses, abnormal enemy patterns, and variable hitstop can diverge. Swarm combat aligns only its single approach target; its capture/finisher sequence is not the normal three-hit rhythm. Gameplay timing, difficulty and hitboxes are unchanged.

For a normal discharge victory the fanfare begins on COMBAT_RESTORE_JUMP, the first phase after the burst. For swarm it begins when the finisher changes to victory. Capture/deflect wins without the burst use actual restoration as their fallback. Failed/retreat encounters resume exploration without a victory phrase. The fanfare continues across map restoration, then exploration fades in over 1.2 seconds. No two BGM tracks overlap, preserving the three-channel limit.

Pause and lifecycle suspension stop music output and retain the native playback position. Mute silences all four channels; logical music time continues while muted so unmuting does not replay an obsolete victory cue. Load/new-game resets playback state. The start menu and field/week transitions stop music. Office entry starts its own track with a 1.2-second fade; return to field replaces it with the existing exploration track. Office dialogue does not restart playback. No save fields or schema were added.

Validation: focused music/entry/lifecycle/data tests, full regression suite, Ruff and spec validation; independent code review. An isolated Web build records its actual canvas and actual WebAudio output (not postmixed). Its fixture uses temporary state and visible automatic successful-combat-input assistance; it does not change combat judgement or production code. Human listening, physical speaker output and every browser/device remain unverified. See the external verification bundle for timestamps and capture details. No commit, push or publication is part of this change.

## Wide-view audio stability

The app enables the renderer's wide_grass_lod mode. Below zoom 1.20 it retains world-even X/Z micro-grass cells plus the existing six-cell shoreline wind cohort; it returns to full detail at zoom 1.30. The gap prevents repeated switching near the boundary. Ground color, objects, actors, event timing and gameplay remain intact; grass decorations are sparser at exploration framing. Close combat framing retains full detail.

In an isolated Chromium 156 / Pyxel 2.9.9 / 48 kHz browser on macOS, the wide-scene median draw cost was 43.6–43.9 ms with 1–3 BGM voices. Reducing voices did not remove measured waveform discontinuities. Micro-grass consumed about 32–34 ms. Stable-cell LOD reduced median draw cost to 24.0–24.4 ms while retaining all three music voices plus SE. An independent pre-codec AudioWorklet detected 102/97 sample steps above 0.01 in repeated matched baseline windows versus 0/0 with LOD. This supports rendering-related audio starvation; it is not a hardware underrun count or subjective listening test.

The normal live camera and water-proximity camera were included in activation testing (zoom 1.0–1.1315). A live normal victory retained the approved music/SE sequence and had zero detected steps in its checked exploration window. Tests cover stable subsets, close-view identity, unchanged player state and hysteresis. iPhone hardware, all-map visual review, full playthrough and physical-speaker listening remain unverified; universal glitch elimination is not claimed.

## Office: two-channel Bach arrangement

The office track is a two-voice reduction of the approved 80 BPM preview of Bach's Prelude in C major, BWV 846. It retains the preview's upper melodic figure and quiet inner harmony exactly, including their native triangle tones, volume envelopes (2/1), 24-second/eight-bar loop and final phrase breath. The preview's brief third bass-root part is omitted, not folded into another channel. Channel 2 is stopped when arriving from a three-voice field track and remains idle during office playback. Channel 3 remains available for effects.

This is the preview's sparse opening reduction, not a performance of the complete prelude: the continuous sixteenths are simplified, the range is folded down, the exposed seventh in bar 3 is softened, and bar 8 settles on C. The source score hash is recorded in bgm_score.json. Historical reference: Bach-Gesellschaft volume 14, Franz Kroll edition, B.W.XIV, printed page 3, BSB bsb11131431 image 47: https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/view/bsb11131431?page=47 . The original study records the bound title/catalogue date 1864 and IMSLP's 1866 date; neither date is a modern arrangement. Composition and historical score are public-domain material. The BSB digital images have a separate NoC-NC designation and are not packaged or redistributed: https://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-NC/1.0/ . No modern recording, online MIDI, external sound sample or digitized score image is included.

Both parts are loaded into native Pyxel sound slots once during setup and played as native loops. There is no streaming decoder, per-note Python callback, extra chord layer or crossfade overlap. Two channels reduce the office voice budget relative to the three-voice preview; they do not by themselves guarantee performance on every device. Pause/background suspension retains native playback position; mute, reset and screen transitions use the same controller as the existing tracks.
